
# Dutch Baby

## Description

This recipe was started in Seattle in the 1900s. It is an American version of a German dish called Pfannkuchen (oven-baked or skillet-cooked egg pancakes). The name Dutch Baby comes from an 'bastardized' version of the word 'Deutsch' and the fact the portions were served in a miniature version. It would be a waste not to spread a receipe with such a cool name.

### Ingredients:
- [ ] 6 large eggs, at room temperature
- [ ] 1 1/2 cups (188g) all-purpose flour
- [ ] 1 1/2 cups (360ml) whole milk, at room temperature (or nondairy)
- [ ] 2 teaspoons lemon zest*
- [ ] 4 Tablespoons (50g) granulated sugar
- [ ] 2 pinches salt
- [ ] 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
- [ ] 6 Tablespoons (86g) unsalted butter
- [ ] 4 Tablespoons confectioners’ sugar, for sifting
- [ ] optional toppings: berries, whipped cream, maple syrup, and/or strawberry sauce (double quantities as needed)

### Steps for preparation:

1. Place the eggs in a blender and blend on medium-high speed until frothy and fluffy, about 45 seconds.
2. Add flour, milk, lemon zest, sugar, salt, and vanilla, and blend until combined. The batter is thin.
3. Set aside and allow to rest for 15 minutes at room temperature, or cover and refrigerate it for up to 24 hours. (No need to bring to room temperature before baking.)
4. Preheat the oven to 400°F (204°C).
5. Divide the butter evenly between two 10-inch oven-safe skillets (or two 3-quart baking dishes), and place them in the oven for just 5 minutes or until melted. (Be careful not to burn it!)
6. Remove the skillets from the oven and use a pastry brush to spread the melted butter up the sides of each pan.
7. Divide the batter evenly between the two hot pans. No need to swirl it around. Place both pans in the oven and bake for 15 minutes.
8. Keeping the Dutch baby pancakes in the oven, reduce the temperature to 350°F (177°C) and bake for 10 more minutes, or until the edges and centers are browned. Rotate the pans if needed for even baking.
9. Remove from the oven and cool for 5 minutes before adding desired toppings. Slice and serve.

The pancakes are best enjoyed right away, but you can cover and store leftovers in the refrigerator for up to 5 days, or in the freezer for up to 2 months.

### Notes:
- [ ] Milk: Best with whole milk, but you can use any milk, dairy or nondairy.
- [ ] Lemon Zest: The lemon zest adds a touch of fresh flavor; the baked pancake doesn’t particularly taste like lemon, though. Add more zest for a stronger flavor, or skip it entirely. Instead, you can add 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon for the doubled recipe.
- [ ] If You Don’t Have a Cast Iron Skillet: You can use any oven-safe skillet that’s around 10 inches in diameter. For 8 servings, use two skillets or two baking dishes rather than overcrowding one pan.
